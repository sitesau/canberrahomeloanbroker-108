The opening conversation is less about numbers and more about framing. A broker will want to understand the purpose of the loan, whether the purchase is a first home, a refinance, an investment or a construction project, because each path carries different documents and decision points. Expect questions about your deposit position, income, existing debts and the type of property you are targeting.

Three things should come out of that first meeting:

  • Whether the broker operates under an Australian Credit Licence or as an authorised credit representative, which you can check against ASIC registers.
  • A Credit Guide, which brokers are required to give you early in the process, setting out how they are paid and what services they offer.
  • A shortlist of which lenders are relevant to your situation, including whether any specialist lenders are worth considering.

If none of those three things happens, that is useful information too. A structured way to prepare for this conversation is set out in this mortgage brokers canberra checklist, which orders the scheme, document and lender-fit questions so nothing gets skipped.

The Document Gathering Stage

After the first conversation, the process shifts to evidence. The broker will ask for identification, income verification, bank statements and details of existing liabilities. What is missing from your file matters as much as what is present, because gaps in documentation are one of the most common reasons pre-approval conditions linger.

A practical way to approach this stage:

  1. Assemble payslips or tax returns, recent statements and a record of savings history before the broker asks.
  2. Flag anything unusual up front, such as casual income, recent job changes or existing guarantees, so the lender choice accounts for it.
  3. Ask the broker which items are still outstanding and what has to be complete before any pre-approval can be relied on for a purchase date.

Investors should also expect sharper questions about how the loan will be structured, including interest-only options and offset accounts, while construction borrowers will be asked about builder contracts and payment stages so progressive drawdowns can be aligned with the build schedule.

Scheme Checks That Can Change Your Cash Position

For eligible buyers, ACT and national schemes can materially alter how much cash is needed at settlement, so expect a broker to test these before recommending a lender. Three schemes sit at the centre of most Canberra conversations: the ACT Home Buyer Concession Scheme, the First Home Guarantee and Help to Buy.

  • From July 2025, eligible buyers pay no stamp duty on properties up to $1,020,000, with concessional rates applying up to $1,455,000 and a maximum saving of $35,238.
  • Eligibility for that concession requires that you have not owned property in Australia in the past five years and that you will live in the property for at least one year.
  • From October 2025, the First Home Guarantee allows a five per cent deposit without lenders mortgage insurance, and Help to Buy may allow a deposit as low as two per cent.

Treat every one of those figures as a prompt to verify during advice rather than a settled outcome. Eligibility, lender acceptance and settlement timing all have to line up before a scheme changes your practical approval path, and not every lender participates in every scheme.

Timelines: From Pre-Approval to Full Approval

Pre-approval commonly arrives within 24 to 48 hours once your documents are in order. It signals that a lender is willing to lend up to a stated amount, subject to conditions, and it lets you bid or make offers with more confidence. It is not a guarantee of final lending.

Full approval typically takes two to three weeks, driven by the volume of documentation, the lender's current workload and any valuation requirements. During this window the lender verifies the details you supplied, assesses the property and converts conditional approval into an unconditional offer.

Two habits make this stage smoother:

  • Do not change your financial position mid-process. New credit applications, large unexplained transfers or a change in employment can stall or undo an approval.
  • Confirm settlement requirements early, including deposit transfer arrangements and any lender conditions, so the dates in your contract remain achievable.

Costs, Commission and What Disclosures to Expect

For standard residential lending, borrowers generally pay nothing for broker services, because the lender pays commission when a loan settles. That does not mean the conversation about money is over. Ask how commission is disclosed for the recommended product and whether any borrower fee applies in your case, particularly if your scenario involves commercial or non-standard lending.

Regulators expect this transparency. ASIC oversees consumer credit in Australia, and the Mortgage and Finance Association of Australia sets professional expectations for broker conduct. ASIC Moneysmart publishes independent consumer information on home loans covering repayments, interest rates and costs, which gives you a benchmark against which to test what you are told.

Finally, document every conversation. Record which lenders were compared, why the recommended option fits your scenario, and how the scheme figures above were verified. Comparing those notes against your own income, expenses, property contract and risk tolerance is the most reliable way to make the broker process work in your favour.

  1. Define your loan purpose. State whether the application is a first purchase, refinance, investment or construction loan, since each brings different documents and decision points.
  2. Prepare your file. Gather income evidence, bank statements and savings history, and flag anything unusual such as casual income or recent job changes.
  3. Verify scheme eligibility. Ask whether ACT or national buyer schemes apply to you, what proof each requires and which lenders accept them.
  4. Track the approval stages. Confirm what must be complete before pre-approval, full approval and settlement, and avoid changing your financial position mid-process.
  5. Review disclosures. Ask how commission is disclosed and whether any borrower fee applies, then document each discussion for comparison.
